# Pyramid skeleton (front owns logic)

Use this structure before Writer drafts prose. Supply facts, sources, and uncertainty; do not ask Writer to invent the logic. The finished document follows [writing.md](../writing.md).

```text
READER: who reads it, in which language, and the question they bring.
ANSWER: one standalone sentence that answers that question directly.
GROUP 1 — <kind of items, e.g. "done: findings">
  POINT 1.1 — <subject + setting + finding with key number + comparison or consequence;
               must make sense read alone; never a topic label>
    Evidence: <facts, numbers, sources, scope limits>
  POINT 1.2 — ...
GROUP 2 — <a different kind, e.g. "not yet verified, but needed">
  POINT 2.1 — <what is unknown and why it matters, stated specifically>
    Evidence: <known facts, sources, and what remains unverified>
GROUP N — decisions for the reader
  DECISION 1 — <the choice, the recommendation, and the reason; cite motivating points>
```

A progress report has exactly three groups: done (findings), not yet verified but needed (open questions parallel to the findings), and decisions for the reader. An open question is its own point, never a meta-item inside the findings group.

Before handing off, check that the ANSWER and every POINT stand alone: subject, setting, finding with the key supplied number, and comparison or consequence. State information rather than a topic label; prefer a complete, slightly longer sentence to an over-compressed one. Do not invent a number for an unmeasured finding or open question. Points inside a group must be the same kind; groups must not overlap; every decision must link to the points that motivate it.
